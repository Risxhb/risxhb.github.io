Priest spell effects

A holy cast circle under the priest, then Lesser Heal, Smite and Barrier of Light, and what happens when a prayer is interrupted. All recordings are frames from the Windows player at half size.

Status: the spell effects were independently approved at 8.3/10 on the fourth review pass. The ability proof passes its automated checks (Editor 22/22, Windows player 20/20) but has not been played by a person.

Spell effects v002 (17 October)

After research into how Final Fantasy VII Remake builds its spells (custom effect modules for beams, ribbons and ground-fitting, and effect-specific image processing), every priest effect was redrawn with one new shader. An original noise texture flows through each layer of light and burns it away with a hot edge. Tubes of light soften toward their outlines. Everything is driven by the battle clock, so a pause freezes the spell exactly. No FF7 assets were used: the game files are encrypted, and only module names and public developer interviews were studied. The approved v001 effects stay in the game until you choose.

Smite v002: approved at 8.1

A faint white thread marks the strike path from the moment the prayer completes. Then a gold shaft drops down it in 83 ms with strands flowing inside, a lopsided crown of shards bursts toward the caster, a thin wave runs along the floor, and embers, wisps and a dark scorch burn out. The strike lands on the floor under the target (tested on a raised dais), follows a moving target until the release and then stays put, and stops under ceilings; under a low ceiling the light bursts through the stone. Three critic passes: 7.4, 7.8, then 8.1 (approved). The last reviewer judged it better than v001 on the same frames; v001 still holds a higher recorded score (8.3), so switching the game over is your call.

The rest of the priest kit v002: approved at 8.1

The cast circle, Lesser Heal and Swift Prayer, Barrier of Light (struck, broken and expiring), Mirror of Light, Shared Light, Benediction, Judgement, Hallowed Ground and Goddess' Mercy (completed and broken), all redrawn the same way with v001's timing kept. Four critic passes: 7.6, 7.9, 7.9, then 8.1 (approved). Along the way: burning edges that first read as brown dirt now glow; floor rings fade cleanly; the barrier got its ribs back and flushes gold when struck; Mirror of Light is a cool silver; Shared Light's barriers are pale blue; the heal column became a hollow tube of light that glows at its outline, so the ally stays visible inside it. The last reviewer scored v002 better than v001 on nine spells and equal on four. Still open, not blocking: the first frame of a shell break is a little milky, and the Mirror's recoil whitens the enemy's armour up close. Each row below: v001 on top, v002 underneath.

  • Every machine check passes (Smite: Editor 18/18, player 16/16; kit: Editor 20/20, player 14/14), including the enemy warning staying readable, broken prayers never releasing, zero memory allocation per frame and no measurable frame-time cost.
  • Not in the game yet: v001 stays live until you choose v002, then a separate integration step.

EarlierGoddess' Mercy

The priest's revival prayer. Light gathers above the fallen ally during the prayer; on completion it comes down, wings of light open behind the ally as they rise, and the pillar outlasts the rise. If the prayer is broken, the light drops, the feathers fall out of the air and the ring goes cold and closes on the body.

  • Independently reviewed at 8.1/10 and approved with no required changes. At your request the reviewer's optional polish was then applied: fuller wings that arrive late in the prayer, beats in the prayer, a smaller priest circle and a cleaner interrupted state. The polished version has not been reviewed again.
  • The Warrior effects (Strike, Guard, Shield Bash, Taunt) are on hold at your word until Eve and Enosh are finished; their first review scored 7.4.
